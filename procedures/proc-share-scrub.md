Unofficial checklist: read everything that travels with a Bot before you share it.

1. Write down [BOT_NAME] and [AUDIENCE]. A public link and access for a defined team are different decisions.
2. Remember what sharing copies: the Bot's identity, description, skills and routines. Your computer and logins are not copied.
3. Read the name, label and picture. Remove anything that names a customer, a person or an internal project.
4. Read the description. A Team Bot has one under About. A personal Bot has none, so read the standing rules you gave it in chat instead.
5. Open each skill. Remove secrets, customer data and internal addresses, including those inside examples and output formats.
6. Open each routine. Remove the same from its sources, matching rules and result destinations.
7. For a Team Bot, list its apps, secrets, skills and files and hold the list against [AUDIENCE]. Everyone who chats with it shares all of them.
8. For each shared connector, ask what the account behind it can reach. Teammates act through that account, including making changes. Share only an account with nothing sensitive.
9. Check that no login, card number or API key sits in a skill, a routine or the description. Logins and cards belong in Vault. API keys go through a secure secret card.
10. Read the approval rules the audience will get. Sending, publishing, purchasing, paying, deleting and production changes should be "Ask first".
11. Know the way back: deleting a Bot also removes the copy shared from it, and its link stops working. Deleting is permanent.
12. Stop here. Share or publish only after every finding above is closed. Keep a Team Bot as a Draft until then.
